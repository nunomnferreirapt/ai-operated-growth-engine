# What stayed human, and why

Running a go-to-market function through agents forces one question early: what may the machine never do on its own? The list we ended up with was short, and it did not change.

**Sending and merging.** Whatever a system drafted (docs changes, chatbot updates, playbook chapters, marketplace replies), a person shipped. Ads and emails went out by hand. Not because the drafts were bad. Because the history matters more than the speed: every change is a pull request someone can open later and read, and that is what made the whole thing possible to hand over.

**Pricing, packaging and partner terms.** The agents brought benchmarks and funnel numbers to the table. The decisions were ours. A price is a position, not a calculation.

**Where the budget went.** A monthly look at the campaign numbers, and the calls that followed. The scorecard proposed. A person decided, with inputs the scorecard did not have.

**Anything a customer would read as our voice.** The chatbot's rules were only ever changed by hand. The same for the sequences people received by email, signed by a person.

The reason it was these four, and not a longer list, is that they are the places where being wrong costs trust rather than time. A bad draft costs an afternoon. A wrong price, a wrong promise to a customer, or a merge nobody can trace costs something the system cannot earn back.

Everything else went to the machine, and the test for that was simple: if the worst case is a proposal nobody accepts, automate it.
