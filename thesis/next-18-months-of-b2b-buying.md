# The next 18 months of B2B buying

Three things were already true for a niche B2B software product by mid-2026, and they will be ordinary by 2028.

**AI assistants refer buyers.** By mid-2026, ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity were sending visitors to the product's website, and some of them became leads. Small in volume, but measurable. The content that earned those referrals was written for that reader: long-form, structured around the questions engineers actually type into an assistant, with claims that can be traced to a source. Answer-engine optimization is a channel; treat it as one while it is still small. How the pieces were produced: [chapter 7](../evidence/case-studies/07-articles-placed-on-industry-platforms.md).

**Attribution breaks.** The assistant is the first touch and leaves no trace the CRM understands. Paid social fills the top of the funnel and almost never appears as the last touch before a deal. Judging channels by last-touch revenue was already wrong in 2026. The honest measure is qualified leads by channel, read monthly, and a willingness to spend on channels that never close anything on their own.

**Agents start buying.** The buyer's assistant will soon read the pricing page, the documentation and the reviews before a human does. A product that publishes its prices, documents itself so a machine can answer about it, and keeps those sources consistent will be recommended. One that hides prices behind a sales call will not be in the comparison.

What we invested in against that: one versioned record of what the company says about its product, with every public surface checked against it; prices on the website; documentation written for self-service; and a CRM that stamps the source at the first touch so the new channels can be seen at all. None of it is new technology. All of it is discipline about what the company says about itself.
