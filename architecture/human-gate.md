# The human gate

No loop posts, merges, sends or publishes on its own. A loop ends in a proposal; if the proposal is accepted, it becomes a pull request; a person merges it, often Nuno himself.

## How it worked

1. **Proposal.** A loop drafts the change (a docs edit, a chatbot update, a playbook chapter, a marketplace reply) with the source it came from.
2. **Pull request.** An accepted proposal becomes a PR on the repository it changes. Every PR follows one structure, so colleagues could read it without knowing how it was produced.
3. **Merge or send.** A person reads, edits or rejects, and merges. Ads went live by hand; the sequence emails were written by people and went out on a schedule.

## Why this shape

The value was the history, more than the approval step: every change to the docs, the chatbot, the playbook or the campaigns is a PR anyone in the team can open and read. It is also what made the system possible to hand over. The format followed the reader: the DevOps and client success teams got pull requests; the marketing coordinator got markdown files to use in their own AI tools.

## What stayed with people

Pricing, packaging and partner terms. Where the budget went, decided in a monthly look at the campaign numbers. Anything a customer would read as the company's voice; the chatbot's rules were only ever changed by hand.

## What broke

A customer-facing agent once reported an email as sent when it never left. Since then, the result of an action like that is checked, and a failed check goes to a person. See [the chatbot chapter](../evidence/case-studies/03-a-chatbot-that-watches-itself.md).
